How to use Mapper3000

Ride tab

  • Use the Ride tab to set your location before planning a route.
  • Tap Track my location (GPS) to let the app follow your position, or choose Set location by tapping map if GPS is unavailable.
  • Pick how many stops to visit and choose a routing style, then tap Find nearest … & route.
  • The app draws a route and shows the best nearby stops from your current location.

All addresses tab

  • View every pin you have added, sorted by order added or alphabetically.
  • Use All, Visited, or Not visited filters to focus on certain stops.
  • Tap any address row to zoom to that location and open its popup on the map.
  • Mark addresses visited or unvisited from the map popup or selection tools.

Add addresses tab

  • Paste one address per line, then tap Add & plot pins.
  • The app tries several geocoders to place each address on the map automatically.
  • If a location cannot be found, it stays in the failed list so you can place it manually on the map.
  • Use Reset all only if you want to clear every saved address and visited state.

Map buttons and map interactions

  • Fit all pins zooms the map to show every stop saved on the device.
  • Select box lets you drag a rectangle across the map and select multiple pins at once.
  • My location centers the map on your current tracked GPS position when it is available.
  • Click a pin to open a popup: mark it visited, edit it, or remove it.
  • Drag the map to explore the area, and scroll or pinch to zoom in and out.
  • Use the sidebar button to hide or show the panel when you want more map space.

Settings

Navigation

Use Bicycling for local stops and garage-sale runs, Walking for short on-foot errands, and Driving or Transit when that matches your trip.

Road Distances

Both are free with an API key. If one is out of quota, switch to the other from this menu without losing anything.

Free at openrouteservice.org. Resets daily. Stored only in this browser.

Regular is the only profile that reliably uses rail-trails and greenways — it happily rides crushed limestone and gravel paths. Road bike sticks to pavement and will route around unpaved trails. Mountain bike is tuned for rough off-road terrain, not smooth trails, so it often prefers streets.

Free at developer.mapquest.com — a one-time free allotment of transactions rather than a daily reset. Stored only in this browser.

MapQuest has one general-purpose Bicycle mode rather than ORS's separate road/mountain/e-bike profiles.

Good future additions

  • Travel time estimates. ORS already returns duration alongside distance — show estimated minutes per leg in the route list so you can plan around opening hours.
  • Stop tags. Label each pin with what's being sold (furniture, clothes, tools, electronics) and filter the map or route by tag.
  • Opening hours per stop. Add a time window to each address; flag stops you might arrive at too early or too late based on your current pace.
  • "Skip for now" status. A third pin state (yellow) to defer a stop without marking it visited — useful when a sale doesn't look worth it but you might circle back.
  • Route export. Copy the ordered stop list to clipboard or download it as plain text so you can hand it to a riding partner or print it.
  • Shareable route link. Encode the current stop list in a URL so you can text it to someone riding with you and they open the same route instantly.
  • Neighborhood radius filter. Draw a circle around your starting point and only route stops within X miles, so the planner ignores pins that are too far out before it even starts.
  • Verified bike-map provider. Add a dedicated bicycle basemap only after confirming its tile URL, usage policy, and reliability across the areas where you ride.
